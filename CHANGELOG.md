# Changelog

All notable changes to atari-os-x86. Versions are git tags (`v0.1.0`); the
image, the boot splash, the System menu's hardware page and init's `status`
reply all show the version they were built from.

## Unreleased

- Closing the lid turns the laptop's panel off (connector DPMS) and pauses the emulator and its sound; opening it brings the picture back with the frame pacing and the just-in-time frame started afresh (the first flips after the wake give no input-lag or wake-up sample). The lid is the ACPI `Lid Switch` evdev device (`EV_SW`/`SW_LID`), read at start too, so a machine booted or an emulator restarted under a closed lid starts paused. A System, Help or Library menu open at the time closes (as with F1/F8/F9 inside it); keys pressed with the lid shut are dropped; USB drives are still mounted and unmounted. Nothing happens with an external monitor driving the picture. Log lines `kms: lid closed, screen off, paused` and `kms: lid open, screen on`
- No webcam and no Bluetooth drivers: the modloop drops all of `drivers/media` but the media controller core (snd-usb-audio needs it) and CEC (the GPU drivers need it), i.e. uvcvideo, V4L2 and videobuf2, plus `drivers/bluetooth` (every HCI driver), the Bluetooth protocols (`rfcomm`, `bnep`, `hidp`, `cmtp`, 6LoWPAN; the bare core `bluetooth.ko` and the chip's own `btrsi` stay because the Redpine RS9113 Wi-Fi driver `rsi_91x`, found in cheap laptops of 2016-2019, links against them; without a stack they do nothing), `drivers/nfc` and `toshiba_bluetooth`, and the remaining Bluetooth firmware (Realtek `rtl8723bs_bt`, Synaptics/Broadcom `.hcd`, 71 KB) and the camera firmware (Intel IPU3/IPU6/IPU7 and Atom ISP, the Visual Sensing Controller: 8.7 MB). Modules 91 to 89 MB (2461 to 2417), firmware 151 to 143 MB, the modloop 201 to 190 MB (i686: 149 MB)
- Wi-Fi firmware for every laptop family with a free driver, not only Intel: Atheros ath10k (QCA6174, QCA9377, QCA988x) and ath9k_htc, Realtek rtlwifi/rtw88/rtw89, Broadcom brcmfmac (PCIe, USB, and the SDIO chips of x86 tablets) and b43's open bits, MediaTek mt76 (MT7921/MT7922/MT7925, MT76x2, MT7601U), plus Realtek Ethernet firmware for r8169. Pruned to what laptops load (no Bluetooth, router, phone or Chromebook files; Intel QuickAssist, Intel Bluetooth and the ath10k router chips dropped): the modloop grows by about 7 MB (194 to 201)
- 32-bit UEFI (Bay Trail/Cherry Trail tablets and netbooks with a 64-bit CPU): the stick carries Limine's `BOOTIA32.EFI` next to `BOOTX64.EFI`, and it boots the same 64-bit kernel (QEMU with 32-bit OVMF: `scripts/qemu-test.sh start uefi32`). The F8 installer and `install-windows.ps1` point the firmware entry at `BOOTIA32.EFI` on such machines
- Input lag one frame less for everyone: every frame waits for the previous frame's vblank before it reads the input (was Low latency only), so Normal mode shows a key at the next vblank, about 1.5 frames (25 ms at 60 Hz) instead of 2.5 (41 ms); same CPU, same sleeps
- Low latency: the just-in-time frame. After the vblank the emulator sleeps until a lead before the next one (95th percentile of the emulate + draw time, plus the vblank's own length and 1.5 ms for the commit, at least 2 ms; half a millisecond more after each late frame, up to 4 ms, one step back after 30 s without one), then reads the input, emulates and flips: about half a frame + the lead (11 ms avg at 60 Hz, was 26). Hardware page `Frame: 0.8 ms emu, lead 3.0, late 0`, `-kms-debug` `jit lead 3.0 ms, late 0`; off by itself after two frames longer than a vblank in a row (`kms: jit off`), until the display settings change; `KMS_JIT=0` turns it off for comparisons. Its sound buffer is 10 ms x 5 (50 ms, was 40: one underrun a minute in QEMU with the writes just before the vblank)
- Sound paced by the display: the Atari makes 59.92 Hz worth of samples per frame but the frames run at the panel's 60.0x Hz, so the device buffer overfilled every 10 to 20 s and the core slept a whole period inside the frame (a repeated picture, and in Low latency a missed vblank that ratcheted the lead up to 13 ms on the ZenBook: 21 ms of lag instead of 11). The ALSA output now resamples by a ratio that follows the fill level, at most 0.5 % (a twentieth of a semitone), so the fill settles where the rates match and the core never waits; no underruns. The Hardware page's `late` counts since the last reset (was the last 2 s), and with `-kms-debug` every late frame logs where its time went (`kms: jit late: ...`)

- A 32-bit image for machines without x86_64 (Core Duo/Solo, Pentium M, Atom N270/N280), experimental and tested in QEMU only: `ARCH=x86` for `alpine-env.sh` (a second environment, `build/alpine-x86/`), the build scripts and `mkimage.sh` give `atari-os-x86-<version>-i686.img` (version `<version>-i686`); non-PAE kernel, BIOS plus `BOOTIA32.EFI` and `BOOTX64.EFI`. `qemu-test.sh` gains `QEMU_SYSTEM`, `QEMU_CPU`, `QEMU_MEM` and a `uefi32` firmware choice. atari800's IDE debug prints no longer use `PRId64` (`%lld` on i686, rejected by `-ansi -pedantic -Werror`)

## v0.1.0 (2026-11-08)

- Boots x86_64 PCs and laptops straight into atari800 from one FAT32 USB stick, BIOS and UEFI (Limine)
- Own PID 1 (`init/init.c`): driver loading by modalias without udev, modloop with the full module and firmware set, hotplug via uevents
- atari800 port without SDL: DRM/KMS video with vsync page flips, ALSA sound, evdev input with hotplug, USB gamepads
- Boot splash with progress lines and an "F8 = System menu" hint for the first boots
- F8 System menu: boot into BASIC, ASH or FujiNet CONFIG; volume and brightness; Wi-Fi scan and connect with diagnostics; SSH on/off and password; hardware status page; save logs to the stick; restart, reboot, shut down
- Reboot straight back into the Atari through UEFI `BootNext`, without ever changing the machine's boot order
- F9 Library: browse, run, mount and copy Atari files across the stick, USB drives and FujiNet's SD folder; recent files, favourites, filter, zip files, multi-disk sets
- USB drives mounted under `/media/usb` for the Library and ASH
- FujiNet-PC built for musl and started next to the emulator over NetSIO; uses the laptop's Ethernet or Wi-Fi; web UI on port 8000
- ASH, the Atari shell, on the stick (cartridge, program, boot disk), with the Tang Nano's SIO $72 file device emulated: `h:` (atari/hdd) and `sd:` (stick and USB drives)
- Ventoy and other image-file boots: init finds `atari-os*.img` on any partition and loop-mounts it as the stick
- Remote access over SSH (dropbear), keys on the stick, off by default
- Network time after DHCP, written back to the RTC
- Headless QEMU test harness (`scripts/qemu-test.sh`) and an unprivileged Alpine build environment (`scripts/alpine-env.sh`)
- Versioned images (`atari-os-x86-<version>.img`), version on the splash, hardware page and `status` reply
- Release pipeline: GitHub Actions build, host `-Werror` build and QEMU smoke boot on every push; releases on `v*` tags
- `scripts/flash.sh`: a safe stick writer for Linux and macOS (removable disks only, type the name, verify by read-back)
- Install onto the laptop's own disk from the F8 menu (whole disk, type ERASE): one FAT32 partition, boots on BIOS and UEFI (firmware entry "Atari" first in the boot order); `atari.media=<serial>` makes the installed copy win over a stick left plugged in, and a booted stick wins over an installed copy
- Every route boots what the user asked for: what is in D1: (or a program, cartridge, tape) boots on any cold start, with ASH's cartridge out and BASIC off for that boot; the boot target (BASIC, No BASIC, ASH, FujiNet CONFIG) applies when nothing is mounted; F8 and the Library "Eject all" go back to it; Library "Run with BASIC"; with FujiNet a local D1: wins over FujiNet's drive
- The Library's first tab is the whole stick (opening in `library/`; FujiNet's SD is `Stick: /fujinet/SD`); Copy to Library, FujiNet SD or a USB drive
- F8 Display: Integer/Fit/Fill scaling, sharp-bilinear smoothing, scanlines, border width, NTSC TV look (atari800's NTSC filter)
- Dual boot next to Linux or Windows: `atari-os-x86-<version>-files.zip` (the stick's files) in every build and release; `scripts/install-linux.sh` adds a GRUB entry through `custom.cfg` only (FAT32 partition, or the image as a file on ext4/btrfs/xfs with GRUB `loopback` and the new `atari.image=<UUID>:<path>`); `scripts/install-windows.ps1` (UEFI) puts the files on a FAT32 partition (existing, or made by shrinking C:) and appends one firmware boot entry, never changing the default; both check Secure Boot, show a plan, ask before writing, and `--remove`/`-Remove` undo the entry
- init: `atari.image=` loopback mode (host filesystem read-write on `/.host`, `status` line `image <dev> <path>`, shown on the hardware page); ext4, xfs and btrfs in the initramfs; at shutdown the modloop and the image's loop device are released first, so `/media` and a host filesystem unmount cleanly
- F1 = Help: every key one per line in a scrolling list (F1-F12 in order, Atari keys, keyboard type with the positional table when it is Atari, joystick, gamepad, Library, menus, power off); the boot hint reads "F1 = Help   F8 = System   F9 = Library" until one of them is opened. atari800's own menu moves to F8 > "Advanced: emulator menu..." (its F8 entry, "System menu", comes back)
- F8 System menu regrouped: Library, Boot into, Eject all, Display..., Sound, Controls..., Network..., Help, Hardware status, Save logs, Restart, Reboot, Shut down, Advanced; TV system and Brightness move into Display, Wi-Fi/IP/SSH into Network; the gamepad's Guide button opens it
- F8 Controls: arrows as cursor keys or joystick 0, keyboard type PC (keys type their caps) or Atari (positional: Shift+2 = `"`, `-` = `<`, `[` = `-`, `'` = `+`, ...), gamepad 1 on joystick 0 or 1, gamepad buttons page (`KMS_KEYBOARD`, `KMS_PAD_SWAP`)
- Shift+arrow keys are cursor keys too (they typed `- = + *`)
- Display: Scaling "Integer+", the largest whole factor on each axis (5 x 4 on 1920x1080): wide, crisp, scanline-friendly
- The build version (`git describe`) is shown at the foot of the F8 System menu and at the top of the F1 Help list
- Module archive (modloop) 414 MB -> 242 MB: Nvidia GSP firmware (RTX 20/30 only) and Intel Wi-Fi ucode of older API versions left out; the newest file per chip stays
- Help: the power-off warning is in inverse video (bit 7 of a character inverts it in the menus, ui_basic.c)
- F8 "Report a problem...": a one-line description plus version, machine, settings, mounted media and the recent emulator/FujiNet logs written to `atari/logs/report-<MMDD-HHMMSS>.txt` on the stick (dmesg and full logs next to it), to attach to a GitHub issue
- F1, F8 and F9 toggle: inside a menu the same key closes it (all levels), another of the three switches to its menu
- F9 Library: a Drives line (what is in D1:-D4:, the cartridge and the tape) opening a popup to eject one of them or all; "Mount as D1:" to "D4:" for disk images
- init checks every FAT before mounting it read-write (`fsck.fat -a -w`, in the initramfs now): the stick at boot ("Checking the stick..." on the splash; `status` line `fsck clean|repaired|failed|skipped`, Hardware page `Stick: FAT clean` / `FAT repaired at boot`) and FAT USB drives when plugged in; lost clusters after an unclean stop are reclaimed, the dirty bit cleared; 60 s at most, then it mounts anyway. A FAT is looked at read-only first, so only ours is checked, never someone else's (a Windows EFI partition)
- F9 Library: copy a whole folder, `[ Copy this folder to Library ]` on a USB drive (any folder, its root too: `library/<label>/`) and `[ Copy this folder to USB... ]` in `library/`; counts first, refuses when short of space, asks; files already there with the same size are skipped; progress `Copying 12/230: name.atr`, Esc stops between files
- F8 Display: Free scaling (horizontal and vertical factors, sliders in 0.05 steps, pixel aspect shown; `KMS_SCALE=FREE`, `KMS_SCALE_X`, `KMS_SCALE_Y`); the Scaling line and the Hardware page show the factors in use (`Fit  3.18 x 3.33`)
- F8 Display: TV look presets Composite, S-Video and RGB monitor plus a Sharpness slider, live (saved in atari800's `FILTER_NTSC_*` lines); the menu line reads `TV look: S-Video, sharp -0.30`
- Module archive (modloop) 242 MB -> 194 MB, kernel modules 143 -> 91 MB (3777 -> 2461): TV tuners and DVB, InfiniBand, MD/device-mapper, FC/SAS/iSCSI host adapters, datacenter NICs, cluster/network and rare filesystems, netfilter and traffic shaping, Xen/vhost/VFIO and NFC left out (`MODLOOP_PRUNE` in `scripts/mkimage.sh`), with modules.dep regenerated by depmod; the build fails if a module the test ZenBook loads (`docs/specs/zenbook-modules.txt`), one of init's base modules or one of a keep-list of old-laptop drivers would go, or if any module would lose a dependency
- Input lag measured, controller to display: evdev event stamps against the page flips' vblank stamps (both monotonic); the Hardware page shows `Input lag: 31 ms avg, 44 max (120)`, `Frame: 1.9 ms emu, 13 ms wait` and the wake-up jitter `Wake: 0.05 ms p50, 0.30 p99, 1.2 max`; `-kms-debug` logs them every 5 s with the sound card's underruns
- Gamepad sticks engage at a quarter of the travel (was half; felt as lag) and release at an eighth
- F8 Controls: Low latency (off by default, an opt-in: it trades quiet fans for speed; `KMS_LOW_LATENCY`): the frame reads its input right after the previous vblank (one frame less), ALSA 10 ms x 4 (40 ms) instead of 20 ms x 4 (80 ms), the main thread `SCHED_FIFO` 10 with locked memory and 50 us timer slack (not in turbo), and the CPU at `energy_performance_preference=performance` through init's new `power fast|quiet` command (status line `power`, Hardware page CPU line `fast`/`quiet`)
- Kernel options: USB HID polled every 1 ms (`usbhid.jspoll=1 usbhid.kbpoll=1 usbhid.mousepoll=1`), Intel panel self refresh off (`i915.enable_psr=0`)
